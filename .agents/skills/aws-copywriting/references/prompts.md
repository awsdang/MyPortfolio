# Frameworks and reusable ChatGPT prompts

These prompts serve Aws’s MyPortfolio project and associated CV, for people considering hiring him. Read the framework needed for the task. Combine the shared prompt brief with the chosen framework instruction to create one standalone prompt. Fill fields using supplied context; leave clearly named fields when creating a reusable template.

## Shared prompt brief

```text
Write [portfolio section or CV entry] for someone considering hiring Aws Abdulfattah. Communicate [main message]. Include [next action] only if the placement needs one. Use [language] and keep it within [length or layout limits].

Write as Aws Abdulfattah. His own writing is personal, professional, confident, and connected. He explains what interests him and links it to his experience and contribution. Use first person for his own work. Preserve the meaning and natural enthusiasm while improving grammar and flow. Do not turn it into slogans or generic sales language.

Use periods and commas for sentence punctuation, retaining contractions such as I'm and I'll. Do not use em dashes, en dashes, middle dots, decorative separators, semicolons, smileys, or emoji. Use plain descriptive headings without numbered chapter styling. Preserve required syntax in code, links, measurements, and names. Personal humor can fit when grounded in Aws’s own examples, but do not force it.

Tone down corporate claims. Explain his personal reason for building, his choices, and his reaction to the result. Project capabilities help a hiring reader understand his contribution. Keep the copy within this personal portfolio/CV purpose; do not redirect it toward selling the project to customers. For a CV, compress the story without losing ownership or factual meaning.

Human-written voice sample:

[paste the approved NotifyX rewrite for a personal project story, or omit this field for other formats]

[paste Aws’s relevant feedback; omit this field if none]

Facts and contribution to use:
[supplied facts, dates where relevant, personal responsibilities, approved results]

Context and available action:
[page or channel, what readers already know, actual destination of any CTA]

Framework instruction:
[insert the selected instruction below]

Treat existing AI-written portfolio copy only as context for facts and layout. Use as voice evidence only the specific excerpts Aws explicitly identified as his own. Do not invent numbers, outcomes, testimonials, or personal responsibilities. If an essential fact is missing or sources conflict, ask a focused question, otherwise omit the unsupported claim.
```

## AIDA. Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Use for a portfolio introduction or contact section leading to a clear next step. Desire means a reason to explore Aws’s work or contact him about a role.

```text
Use AIDA. Open with the most relevant truthful reason for this reader to pay attention. Build interest through the supplied experience or work. Show what that experience could contribute to the reader’s needs, using evidence rather than guarantees. End with an invitation to [available next action]. Keep the tone compatible with Aws’s sample; do not add hype to make the framework more obvious.
```

## PAS. Problem, Agitation, Solution

Use when a project addresses a specific, documented problem. Agitation explains real consequences; it must not manufacture fear or make a hiring reader feel inadequate.

```text
Use PAS. Describe [documented problem] in concrete terms. Explain its actual consequence from [supplied evidence], without exaggerating or inventing costs. Describe Aws’s specific contribution to the solution and the supported outcome. Add [next action] only if it fits the section. If evidence for the consequence is absent, keep that stage brief and factual.
```

## BAB. Before, After, Bridge

Use for project summaries and transformations with a supported before/after state.

```text
Use Before, After, Bridge. Describe [verified starting state], then [verified resulting state], then explain the work Aws personally did to connect them. Distinguish team outcomes from his own responsibilities. If the result is an intention rather than an observed outcome, say so. Keep the narrative clear and natural.
```

## FAB. Features, Advantages, Benefits

Use to translate technical work into significance for a nontechnical reader. Do not assume every engineering detail has a measurable business outcome.

```text
Use Features, Advantages, Benefits. For [supplied capability], explain what it does, why that behavior is useful, and what it means for [audience]. Keep only technical details that help the reader understand the contribution. Clearly distinguish a demonstrated benefit from an intended one. Write connected prose unless a list better fits the requested layout.
```

## Four Ps. Picture, Promise, Prove, Push

Use only when a portfolio contact section benefits from this structure. A promise describes Aws’s approach or contribution to a team. Do not invent a service offer or a guaranteed hiring outcome. This framework is optional and usually unnecessary for a project story or CV entry.

```text
Use Picture, Promise, Prove, Push. Describe the relevant situation the reader wants to achieve. State the contribution or approach Aws can offer without guaranteeing unverified results. Support it with [provided evidence]. End with a clear, proportionate invitation to [available next action]. Avoid invented urgency or scarcity.
```

## STAR. Situation, Task, Action, Result

Use for recruiter-focused project accounts and case studies where personal ownership matters.

```text
Use Situation, Task, Action, Result. Establish the project context, Aws’s responsibility, the specific actions he took, and the supported result. Preserve the distinction between what he did and what the team achieved. If there is no measured result, use a supplied qualitative outcome or flag the missing result separately. Keep it readable as a short story rather than an interview worksheet.
```

## Personal narrative. Motivation, Experience, Contribution

Use for an About section or short bio. This is a practical structure drawn from Aws’s sample, not a named industry framework.

```text
Structure this around what interests Aws, the relevant experience that shaped his work, and what he contributes to a team today. Let the selected facts connect those ideas. Preserve his first-person, positive professional voice. Do not invent a dramatic origin story, a quotation, or a career lesson he has not supplied. Use a CTA only when the section needs one.
```

## Editing prompt

Use this instead of forcing a persuasion framework on copy that already has the right structure.

```text
Rewrite the draft below using Aws’s human-written voice sample and confirmed preferences from the shared brief. Improve grammar, flow, and clarity while preserving every supported fact and ownership distinction. The draft is AI-written, so its style is not a voice reference. Keep [layout/length limits]. Return one usable rewrite; explain only any material factual ambiguity.

Draft:
[paste the selected portfolio text]
```
