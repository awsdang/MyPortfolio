FAMILY HISTORY · AI RECOGNITION · AI-GENERATED ART

A family tree that starts with old photos and ends on the wall.

The app helps build the tree. The final result becomes a printed piece made for that family.

I built Asheerty as a complete consumer product: the mobile app, backend, dashboard, order flow and fulfilment tools. I am now building the full technical cycle around the tree.

  1. 01Data entryThe user adds names, relationships, photos, documents and old family trees.
  2. 02AI tree readingAI reads old family trees and other family material, finds connections and builds a first version of the tree inside the app.
  3. 03User reviewThe user checks and corrects the people and relationships before they are saved to the database.
  4. 04The living treeThe tree stays interactive and keeps changing as the family updates it.
  5. 05AI custom artworkAI helps turn the reviewed tree into an organic composition, then we refine it and prepare it for print.

The full cycle

AI is used at both ends of the process. First, it reads old family trees and other family material, finds names and connections, and builds an initial tree inside the app. The user reviews and corrects the result before the people and relationships are stored in the database.

The reviewed tree becomes the input for a second AI process. AI creates the layout and shapes it into an organic tree. I then refine the family-specific artwork and prepare the final version for printing.

NOTIFYX · NOTIFICATION INFRASTRUCTURE

I got tired of rebuilding notifications, so I built NotifyX.

One API, one dashboard and one place to see what failed.

What it handles

Every product needed the same mix of providers, queues, retries, scheduling and delivery status. I stopped copying the setup and built it as its own system. NotifyX now handles FCM, APNS, HMS and Web Push for five companies, with each worker benchmarked at about 150 messages per second. The code is public at github.com/awsdang/notifyx.

LIVE VIEW · HOW A MESSAGE MOVES 0 delivered · 0 retried

Every dot is a message. If a provider fails, the message moves into the retry path and tries again.

What stayed with me

HOW I WORK

Five things I keep coming back to.

  1. Find the real problem, not the first one.

    Sometimes it is code. Sometimes it is pricing, UI, process or a decision nobody has made yet. I try to understand that before I start building.

  2. Change live systems in small steps.

    Most of my major rebuilds happened while people were still using the product. I plan carefully, ship smaller changes and keep a way back.

  3. Design for slow networks and distracted people.

    Small bundles, fewer round trips, useful loading states and clear language. These details matter more than they look.

  4. Make payment systems boring.

    States, retries, idempotency, callbacks and reconciliation. Boring is good when money is moving.

  5. Build it so the team can run it without me.

    I like ownership, but I do not want to become a bottleneck. Clear systems and people who understand them are the real handoff.
